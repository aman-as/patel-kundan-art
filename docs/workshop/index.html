The workshop

Two brothers, one bench, in Karol Bagh, Delhi.

Patel Kundan Art is a family workshop in Karol Bagh. Sunil Singh takes the orders and finishes the pieces; his younger brother Anil Singh does the kundan setting. The work is not sent out.

What we do

We manufacture kundan and jadau jewellery by hand, to order, for retail jewellers and for individuals. Kundan setting is the specialisation — it is the slowest, most particular stage of making a piece, and it is the one that decides whether the finished jewellery holds together.

We are manufacturers, not a shop. There is no window and no stock on display. Everything that leaves the bench was ordered by someone first.

How a job runs

Six stages, and a written record at both ends.

01/ 06

  1. 01

    Design or reference

    A photograph on WhatsApp, a drawing, or a piece to copy. Most jobs start with a photograph.

  2. 02

    Weight and estimate

    We tell you what the piece needs and what the work involves before anything is committed.

  3. 03

    Gold received

    Weighed and entered in the job register, in front of you.

  4. 04

    Ghaat — the framework

    The gold structure of the piece is built by hand.

  5. 05

    Kundan setting

    Stones set in the traditional way. This is the stage the workshop is named for.

  6. 06

    Finish and weigh out

    Polished and checked, then weighed out and delivered against the same register it came in on.

The job register

Every gram is written down, in and out.

When somebody hands over gold — a retailer sending a month's work, or a family bringing a set to be remade — the first thing that happens is that it is weighed and written into the job register. When the finished piece goes back, it is weighed against the same entry.

This is ordinary practice and it should not be remarkable. It is worth saying because it is the actual question behind every new relationship in this trade, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a reassurance.
